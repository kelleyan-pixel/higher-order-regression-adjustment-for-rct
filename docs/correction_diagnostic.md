# Superpopulation correction: diagnostic notes

This note accompanies Section 3.2 and Appendix E of the paper. All numbers are generated by the
pipeline; see `results/correction_summary.{json,csv,md}` (all 39 designs, 95% level) and
`results/calibration.csv` (three representative designs, nominal levels 80-99%, with calibration
errors and Monte Carlo standard errors).

## Question

Under superpopulation sampling the variance of IREG for the population ATE contains a first-order
component `G/n`, `G = (beta1 - beta0)' Sigma_X (beta1 - beta0)`, which the sample-centred robust
variance omits. The feasible correction adds the plug-in `delta_hat' Sigma_hat_X delta_hat / n`.
When is a coverage change produced by the plug-in evidence that the estimand-level component is
being recovered?

## Method

Every design for which the repository reports uncorrected coverage (the paper's 18 gamma = 0
designs, the introductory example and its heavy-tailed variant, and 19 supplementary designs) is rerun on exactly the same simulated datasets. On each dataset the same IREG estimate and
degrees of freedom give intervals with (i) the standard HC variance, (ii) the HC variance plus the
plug-in, and (iii) the HC variance plus the true `G/n` (an oracle, infeasible in practice). Coverage
is computed for the PATE at nominal levels 80, 90, 95, 98 and 99%, changing only the t critical
value. The uncorrected 95% coverages reproduce the previously reported numbers exactly.

## Findings

- **Large heterogeneity (R2_tau = 0.69).** The plug-in averages G/n (ratio 1.00); the feasible and
  oracle intervals agree and are close to nominal at every level; HC1 alone undercovers badly.
- **Small heterogeneity (R2_tau between 0.003 and 0.022).** Adding the true G/n changes coverage by
  about a point or less; the plug-in averages several to 31 times G/n under skewed or heavy-tailed
  covariates and can change coverage by much more.
- **No heterogeneity (gamma = 0, G = 0 exactly).** The oracle interval equals the uncorrected one,
  but the plug-in moves HC1 coverage close to 95% in skewed designs. Across nominal levels the
  plug-in interval is not calibrated: it undercovers at 80% and slightly overcovers at 98-99%, so its
  agreement with nominal at 95% is a crossing point. Combined with HC3 it overcovers at every level.
  All of its effect in these designs is finite-sample inflation from estimation noise in delta_hat.

## Implication for the paper

The component G/n matters, and its plug-in is accurate, when covariate-explained heterogeneity is
large. When heterogeneity is small or absent, coverage improvements from the plug-in should not be
read as recovery of the estimand-level component. The paper therefore reports the standard
implementation in its main inference tables and presents the correction as a caveat with a
diagnostic, not as a proposed method.
